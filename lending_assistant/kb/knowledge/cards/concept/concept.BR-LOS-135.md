**In short:** Sampling rates can be set globally, per partner and per product; changes need maker-checker approval, are effective-dated and are kept in a change history of six facts.

## What the rule says

BR-LOS-135 (Must, BRD 6.5, PDF page 36) says sampling rates are configurable globally, per partner and per product. Changes require maker-checker approval, are effective-dated, and are retained in a change history recording before value, after value, maker, checker, timestamp and stated justification. This is an origination rule (the LOS side of the BRD), not a servicing rule.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD doesn't say why; the usual reason is that the rate decides how much oversight the Bank has, so a change must be traceable and approved by someone other than the person who proposed it.

## Example

Credit Risk proposes to lower Partner A's rate from 15% to 10%, starting on the 1st, with a reason. A second officer approves. The history shows the old and new values, both names, the time and the reason.

## Terms to know

- **Maker-checker:** one person proposes and another approves. See `glossary.maker-checker`.
- **Effective-dated:** the change starts on a stated date.
- **Change history:** a record of each change and who made and approved it.
- **Justification:** the written reason for the change.
