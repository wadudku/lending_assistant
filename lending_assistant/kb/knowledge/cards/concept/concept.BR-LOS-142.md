**In short:** A reviewer can defer an application to a supervisor with a note, instead of being forced to accept or reject.

## What the rule says

BR-LOS-142 (Must, BRD 6.5.1, PDF page 37) says a reviewer can defer an application to a supervisor with a note, rather than being forced to accept or reject. This is an origination rule (the LOS side of the BRD), not a servicing rule.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD doesn't say why; the usual reason is that a reviewer should not have to guess on a case they are unsure about, and the 6.5.1 introduction asks for a defensible decision on each case (PDF page 36).

## Example

A reviewer cannot tell whether the stated income is realistic. They defer the case to a supervisor with the note “income looks high for this district”. The supervisor then accepts or rejects it.

## Terms to know

- **Defer:** pass a decision to someone else.
- **Supervisor:** a senior staff member who handles deferred cases.
- **Note:** a short written comment attached to the case.
- **Queue:** the list of cases waiting for a reviewer.
