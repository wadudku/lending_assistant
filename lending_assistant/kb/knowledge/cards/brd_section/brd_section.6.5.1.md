**In short:** Section 6.5.1 describes the review screen for a sampled application, with seven panels, and holds six rules for rejection reasons, records, deferral, the queue, reversal and quality figures.

## What this section says

BRD 6.5.1 (PDF pages 36 and 37) says the review screen must be designed for throughput: a reviewer working a queue of several hundred applications must reach a defensible decision on each in well under a minute, without leaving the screen. A table describes seven panels: applicant, account, request, score, flags, partner context and identity. Six requirements follow, BR-LOS-140 to BR-LOS-145: a rejection needs a reason code from a list (140); each decision is saved with who, what, why and when (141); a reviewer may pass a case up to a supervisor with a note (142); the queue is driven from the keyboard and shows how many remain (143, a Should); only a supervisor can reverse a decision, and the original stays visible (144); and sampling outcomes feed partner quality figures and can raise a partner's rate (145).

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD gives the reason in the section introduction: the review screen must be designed for throughput, so that a reviewer facing several hundred applications can reach a defensible decision on each in well under a minute (PDF page 36).
