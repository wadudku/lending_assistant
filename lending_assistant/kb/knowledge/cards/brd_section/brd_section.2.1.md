**In short:** Section 2.1 names three constraints of the Bank's present lending operation: acquisition cost, document-bound origination and spreadsheet-based servicing.

## What this section says

BRD 2.1 (PDF page 14) says the Bank's present lending operation carries three structural constraints that the programme is meant to remove. First, acquisition cost per loan: reaching agricultural and rural borrowers through the branch network costs more than the interest on a loan of this size, so the product is not viable at volume through branches alone. Second, manual, document-bound origination: intake, verification and approval depend on physical forms and branch visits; turnaround is measured in days, credit decisions are hard to reproduce for audit, and the evidence is held across files. Third, servicing dispersed across spreadsheets and manual process: schedule generation, accrual, classification and provisioning depend on spreadsheet work and manual reconciliation, which limits portfolio size, delays regulatory reporting and makes it hard to show Bangladesh Bank a consistent basis for classification and provisioning.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD gives the reason in the section itself: these three constraints are what the programme is intended to remove (PDF page 14).

## How it meets Frappe Lending

This section holds no requirement row, so there is nothing in it to compare with Frappe Lending, and the team has made no statement about it. It also describes the origination system (LOS), which the team has not analysed.
