**In short:** A wider change to the financial terms of a loan, which may include the rate, tenor, instalments, capitalisation or settlement.

## Plain meaning

The BRD defines restructuring as a broader modification of financial terms, potentially including rate, tenor, installments, capitalisation or settlement (BRD 1.5). Rescheduling is the narrower case.

## Example

Rahim cannot keep up with his EMI. The bank adds his unpaid interest to the principal (capitalisation), lowers the rate and stretches the tenor. A new schedule replaces the old one, and the old one is kept.

## Why it matters here

BR-LMS-183 asks that a restructure generates a new schedule and keeps the prior one as superseded; the verified result is covered. BR-LMS-184 asks for restructuring limits by branch and period, which the verification found missing. See `decision.restructure` for the approval side.
