**In short:** Screens must be easy to use with one hand, with buttons big enough to press while the officer is on their feet.

## What the rule says

BR-LOS-154 (Must, BRD 6.7.1, PDF page 40) says the application shall be "operable one-handed, with touch targets sized for use while standing". It is a usability rule about the layout of the app: controls within reach of one thumb, and buttons large enough to hit without care.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD gives the operating context for all of section 6.7 (PDF page 39): field officers work at gatherings arranged by the partner, often in rural places with unreliable mobile data. They stand rather than sit, work under time pressure with a queue of applicants waiting, and serve applicants who may have limited literacy. The BRD says every requirement in this section "follows from that operating context", so this rule comes from officers who stand and work fast with a queue waiting.

## Example

An officer holds the device in one hand while standing, and moves from the applicant list to the next screen with a thumb. No small link needs two hands or a careful tap.

## Terms to know

- **Touch target:** the part of the screen the officer touches, sized for use while standing.
- **One-handed:** the BRD's wording: the app is operable one-handed.
