**In short:** Officers can switch the app between Bangla and English at any time without losing the work they have started.

## What the rule says

BR-LOS-153 (Must, BRD 6.7.1, PDF page 40) says the interface shall support Bangla and English, "switchable by the user without losing in-progress work". Two things matter: both languages are offered, and a switch in the middle of a verification must not discard what the officer has already entered or captured.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD gives the operating context for all of section 6.7 (PDF page 39): field officers work at gatherings arranged by the partner, often in rural places with unreliable mobile data. They stand rather than sit, work under time pressure with a queue of applicants waiting, and serve applicants who may have limited literacy. The BRD doesn't say why both languages are needed; the usual reason is that officers and applicants read different languages, and an officer may switch to show the applicant a screen in their own language.

## Example

An officer starts a verification in English, then turns the screen to Bangla to let the applicant read their own details. The signature already drawn and the fields already filled are still there after the switch.

## Terms to know

- **Bangla:** the national language of Bangladesh.
- **In-progress work:** work the officer has started and not finished.
