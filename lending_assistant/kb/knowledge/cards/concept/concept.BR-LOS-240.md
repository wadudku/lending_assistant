**In short:** The platform presents one dashboard of the origination pipeline: volume and value by status, funnel conversion and drop-off, ageing, a partner league table and field officer utilisation.

## What the rule says

BR-LOS-240 (Must, BRD 8.1, PDF page 57) says the platform presents an operational dashboard. It shows five things: pipeline volume and value by status; funnel conversion and drop-off; ageing within status; a partner league table; and field officer utilisation.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD gives one general reason for the whole of Section 8: “Reporting is not a by-product of this programme; for several stakeholders it is the programme. Credit Risk, Finance, Compliance and Internal Audit each need a view that the current process cannot produce reliably at volume.” (PDF page 57). The BRD doesn't say why this row asks for a dashboard rather than a report; the usual reason is that the people who run the pipeline need to see where applications are waiting without asking for a special report.

## Example

On Monday a manager opens the dashboard. It shows 40 applications waiting for review, worth 6 million in total, and 12 of them have waited more than five days in that status. A second table ranks the partners by the applications they sent, and a third shows how many visits each field officer has on their list.

## Terms to know

- **Pipeline:** the applications that have been started and not yet finished or turned down.
- **Funnel conversion and drop-off:** how many applications move from one stage to the next, and how many leave at each stage.
- **Ageing within status:** how long an application has stayed in its current status.
- **League table:** a ranking, here of partners.
- **Utilisation:** how much of a person's available capacity is in use.
