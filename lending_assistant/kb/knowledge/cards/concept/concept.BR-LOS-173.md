**In short:** The officer captures the applicant's signature on a full-screen canvas with stylus or finger, and it is stored as an image with a time and coordinates.

## What the rule says

BR-LOS-173 (Must, BRD 6.7.3, PDF page 41) says a wet signature "shall be captured on a full-screen canvas supporting stylus and finger input, with clear and retry". It is stored "as an image with capture timestamp and coordinates". The applicant can clear and sign again, and the stored signature carries the time and the place it was taken.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD doesn't say why; the usual reason is that a signature taken on the spot at the meeting is evidence that this applicant was present and agreed, and the time and place stored with it make the evidence checkable later.

## Example

An applicant signs with a finger on the full-screen canvas, is not satisfied, taps clear and signs again. The accepted signature is saved as an image with the time it was taken and the coordinates of the place.

## Terms to know

- **Wet signature:** the BRD's term for a signature drawn by hand, here on the screen.
- **Canvas:** the signing area of the screen.
- **Coordinates:** the geographic position recorded with the capture.
