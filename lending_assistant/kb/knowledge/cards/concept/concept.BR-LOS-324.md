**In short:** The credit approver can sanction as proposed, sanction at revised terms, sanction subject to conditions, defer to a higher authority with a note, or decline; a decline and a revision each need a reason code from a list.

## What the rule says

BR-LOS-324 (Must, BRD 6.6, PDF page 38) says the credit approver can sanction as proposed, sanction at revised terms, sanction subject to conditions, defer to a higher authority with a note, or decline. A decline and a revision each require a reason code from a configurable list; free text is additional, never a substitute. This is an origination rule (the LOS side of the BRD), not a servicing rule.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD doesn't say why; the usual reason is that a credit decision is rarely only yes or no, and coded reasons for a decline or a change let the Bank explain and count them. See also BR-LOS-305 (PDF page 34).

## Example

An approver reduces a 200,000 request to 150,000, picks the reason code “Income supports a lower instalment”, and sanctions at the revised terms.

## Terms to know

- **Revised terms:** an amount, tenor or rate different from the proposal.
- **Conditions:** things the borrower must satisfy before the loan is approved. See BR-LOS-327.
- **Defer:** pass the decision to someone with higher authority.
- **Reason code:** a short coded reason picked from a list.
