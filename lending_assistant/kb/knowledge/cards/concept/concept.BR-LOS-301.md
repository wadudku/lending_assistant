**In short:** The scorecard can draw on application data, derived ratios, geography, account behaviour, the partner's track record, credit bureau data and duplicate, negative-list and mandatory-sampling flags.

## What the rule says

BR-LOS-301 (Must, BRD 6.4, PDF page 33) says the scorecard can draw on, at minimum: application data including demographics, declared income, loan purpose, requested amount and tenor; derived measures such as instalment-to-income and loan-to-income; geography at district and upazila level; account behaviour where available from the CBS; the partner's own track record; credit bureau data where the inquiry has been performed at this stage; and duplicate, negative-list and mandatory-sampling flags. This is an origination rule (the LOS side of the BRD), not a servicing rule.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD doesn't say why; the usual reason is that a fair credit opinion needs the borrower, the loan, the place, the account and the partner's history together.

## Example

For one application the scorecard reads the borrower's age and income, the instalment as a share of income, the district, the account's recent activity and the partner's past loans, and notes that the NID is on a negative list.

## Terms to know

- **Derived measure:** a figure worked out from others, such as instalment divided by income.
- **Upazila:** a local administrative area below district.
- **Negative list:** a list of people the Bank will not lend to.
- **Credit bureau data:** a person's credit history held by a bureau.
