**In short:** Section 2.3 lists nine business objectives, OBJ-01 to OBJ-09, each with an owner.

## What this section says

BRD 2.3 (PDF pages 14 and 15) has a table of nine objectives. OBJ-01: originate high-volume, low-value loans through aggregator partners at a viable marginal cost (Retail / MSME business). OBJ-02: keep real credit judgement over a volume too large to review individually, through random and rule-based sampling (Credit Risk). OBJ-03: apply a consistent, versioned and reproducible scorecard to every application (Credit Risk). OBJ-04: one retrievable, immutable evidence record for every lending decision (Internal Control and Compliance). OBJ-05: eliminate duplicate and misdirected disbursement through account validation before submission and again before funding (Operations). OBJ-06: service the funded book without a proportional increase in back-office headcount (Operations / Finance). OBJ-07: classify assets and calculate provisions on one configurable basis aligned to Bangladesh Bank rules, with a complete trail (Credit Risk / Finance). OBJ-08: produce Bangladesh Bank agricultural and rural credit returns and monthly CIB contribution from system data (Compliance / Finance). OBJ-09: reconcile the platform's position against the Core Banking System every day, with zero unexplained differences carried overnight (Finance).

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD doesn't say why; the usual reason is that a programme needs named objectives with owners so that each can be tracked to a person (PDF page 14).

## How it meets Frappe Lending

This section holds no requirement row, so there is nothing in it to compare with Frappe Lending, and the team has made no statement about it. Objectives OBJ-06 to OBJ-09 concern servicing; OBJ-01 to OBJ-05 concern the origination system, which the team has not analysed.
