**In short:** Section 6.5 holds eight rules for sampling: picking by a controlled random rate, a sound random source, a reproducible record, rate settings, system-decision records and mandatory rules.

## What this section says

BRD 6.5 (PDF pages 35 and 36) calls sampling the mechanism by which the Bank keeps credit judgement over a volume too large to review one by one. It holds eight requirements, BR-LOS-130 to BR-LOS-137: after scoring, a share is picked for manual review (130); the pick uses a sound random source (131); the seed and rate are saved so a pick can be reproduced (132); the rate comes from product, then partner, then global default (133); applications not picked advance as a system decision (134); rates are set globally, per partner and per product with a second approver (135); the rate screen shows how much review work the rate creates (136, a Should); and some applications are always reviewed under mandatory rules (137).

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD gives the reason in the section introduction: sampling's integrity (that selection is genuinely random, that the rate is controlled, that decisions are attributable and reproducible) is the control that makes partner-sourced origination defensible to internal audit and to the regulator (PDF page 35).
