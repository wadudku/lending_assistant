**In short:** Section 2.2 says partner-sourced origination makes the product viable and the servicing system lets the Bank handle the volume, so the two are built together.

## What this section says

BRD 2.2 (PDF page 14) says Bangladesh Bank sets annual agricultural and rural credit disbursement targets for scheduled banks. Partner-sourced origination moves customer acquisition to organisations that already hold the borrower relationship, and the LOS lowers the Bank's marginal processing cost per loan to where the product is viable. The LMS lets the resulting book be serviced at that volume without a proportional increase in back-office headcount, and be classified, provisioned and reported on one consistent basis. The two depend on each other: origination without servicing creates a book the Bank cannot administer, and servicing without origination has nothing to administer. They are therefore specified, governed and phased together.

## Why the bank wants it

The BRD gives the reason in the section itself: the regulator's annual targets, the need for a viable cost per loan, and the dependence of origination and servicing on each other (PDF page 14).

## How it meets Frappe Lending

This section holds no requirement row, so there is nothing in it to compare with Frappe Lending, and the team has made no statement about it. It also describes the origination system (LOS), which the team has not analysed.
